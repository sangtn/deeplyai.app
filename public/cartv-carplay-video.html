Video in CarPlay: which cars, iOS 26.4 and 27.2, and what still works

Updated November 20, 2026

Since iOS 26.4, Apple lets video apps show a picture on the car screen. A lot of what you read online about it is wrong or out of date. This page explains what is true in November 2026, and how to check your own car.

In short

  • Video in CarPlay needs iOS 26.4 or later and a car whose maker has turned it on.
  • It plays only while the car is parked. When the car moves, the picture stops and the sound continues.
  • As of November 2026, no car maker has publicly announced support. Check your car with Car check in Car.TV, or ask your car maker.
  • iOS 27.2 stopped the mirroring and fake-receiver tricks some apps used to show video in any car. Car.TV never used them.

What Apple added in iOS 26.4

iOS 26.4 added a new kind of CarPlay app: a video app. On a car that supports it, the app can play video on the car’s own display while the car is parked, using CarPlay’s standard screens. When you drive off, CarPlay removes the picture and keeps the sound. This is decided by iOS and the car, not by the app.

Whether a car offers video to apps is up to its maker. The car tells the iPhone when it connects. A car with CarPlay doesn’t automatically have video in CarPlay. In cars that don’t offer video, iOS doesn’t show video apps on the car screen at all.

Which cars support video in CarPlay?

As of November 20, 2026, we have found no public announcement from any car maker that its cars support video in CarPlay. That is why this page lists no cars. Lists online that name “supported” models without a source from the car maker should be read with care.

When a car maker announces support, we will add it here with the date and a link to its announcement.

How to check your car

  1. Update iOS to 26.4 or later on your iPhone.
  2. Connect to CarPlay once with Car.TV installed. If your car supports video, Car.TV appears on the car screen and, on your iPhone, Car.TV › Settings › Car check confirms it; it also tells you if iOS needs an update first. Car check is free. If Car.TV never appears on the car screen, your car doesn’t offer video in CarPlay.
  3. Ask your car maker or dealer whether your model and its current software support “video in CarPlay”. Some cars may need a head-unit software update.
  4. Expect video only while parked. If the picture goes away when you drive, that is CarPlay working as designed, not a fault.

What changed in iOS 27.2

Before iOS 27.2 (released in late October 2026), some apps showed video in almost any CarPlay car with workarounds: mirroring the phone screen, pretending to be an AirPlay or DLNA receiver, or drawing a web browser on the car screen. Since iOS 27.2, many users of those apps report only sound, or a message that the car doesn’t support video.

Car.TV doesn’t rely on any of those tricks. It uses Apple’s official CarPlay video. The trade-off is honest: Car.TV shows video on the car screen only in cars whose maker has enabled it, and only while parked.

What still works, whatever your car

  • Listening while you drive: in cars with video in CarPlay, Car.TV keeps the sound going on the car screen. In other cars, start playback on the iPhone before you set off; the iPhone’s sound plays through the car as usual.
  • Car Mode on iPhone: a big, simple player for passengers, or for you while charging or waiting.
  • Downloads for the road, so a weak mobile signal doesn’t stop the film.
  • Casting to the TV when you arrive.

What doesn’t work, and why

  • Netflix, YouTube, Disney+ and other streaming services on the car screen. They protect their videos with DRM and their terms don’t allow other apps to play or mirror them. Use your own sources, or the official app on a passenger’s device.
  • Video while driving. CarPlay never shows it, in any car, with any app.
  • Car.TV on the screen of a car without video in CarPlay. Apple hides CarPlay video apps there.
  • Channels from us. Car.TV is a player and sells no channels; you add your own.
